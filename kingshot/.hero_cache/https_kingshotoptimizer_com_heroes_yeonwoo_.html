Strat Game Sloth Strategic Summary

The main function of Yeonwoo is in Expedition mode, where she is categorized by Kingshot as an elite growth hero, providing an essential permanent buff to accelerate your account’s tech research speed by up to 15% (additive) with Well-Traveled. As if that wasn't enough, her primary expedition skill, On Guard, grants a massive 25% army-wide lethality buff, which identical to Chenko’s first skill. This makes her an absolute staple for any alliance member to build, as she is one of the few heroes who can provide this lethality boost in rallies to increase damage during Bear Hunt as well as offensive PvP content. All of this is available in a free-to-play farmable hero. Strategic players should prioritize Yeonwoo with their epic general shards to reach at least 4 stars to max her abilities, especially since she requires 30 fewer shards to unlock than Chenko while providing more total utility. She will lose out to Chenko is solo march combat, however, since Chenko has 2 combat skills to Yeonwoo's single combat skill. For Conquest and Arena mode, Yeonwoo is a premier archer class hero who excels as a powerful area-of-effect (AoE) damage dealer, acting as a superior choice to Diana for clearing out opposing fields when her star level is fully built. Her signature ability, Petalbloom, utilizes a multi-phase sequence that delivers two single-target strikes followed by a significant 210% AoE blast that reliably wipes out opposing escort troops. She further enhances her tactical performance with a passive 24% attack buff, allowing her to outclass just about every other starter purple hero in field-wide damage dealing potential. Because players will naturally prioritize her development for research and lethality benefits, she often reaches competitive power levels faster than other Conquest damage dealers as well. Savvy players should position her in the backline to maximize her uptime and ability cycles, where she will remain a cornerstone of free-to-play tactical formations through at least the first few generations of heroes.

Hero Story

Story I
Yeonwoo is a passionate swordswoman, admired by all for her meticulous nature and sharp eye. She has an uncanny ability to see things from a unique perspective, likely due to her extensive travels and broadened horizons. Whether friend or foe, it is wise to always stay on high alert when you are around Yeonwoo.
Story II
"Accomplishing something is hard, but ruining it is easy." This is the principle Yeonwoo has lived by her whole life. During her journeys, she saw many abandon their convictions in pursuit of short-term gain. Most people understand the value of perseverance, but few truly practice it. "A blade must be honed a thousand times to be sharp, and a dance takes a lifetime of dedication to master." Yeonwoo weaves this principle of swordsmanship into every aspect of her daily life.
Story III
Yeonwoo first held a sword at the age of five—a ceremonial blade that stood taller than she did. While other children were still busy playing and roughhousing, she rose early each morning, practicing her stance with quiet determination. She tirelessly repeated the routine day after day, each session lasting for a few hours. It wasn't until a year later that her master, who was also her father, finally taught her the most basic moves. At that moment, no one could have foreseen the remarkable things she would go on to achieve in the future.
Story IV
Yeonwoo trains in autumn to absorb the spirit of fallen petals and races through stormy nights to emulate the grace of swallows. She incorporates everything she has seen and heard into her dance. Each time she executes her moves, she reconnects with her beliefs and purpose. Each time she sheaths her sword, she quiets her restlessness with calm. Yeonwoo believes that the vast insights gained from her travels, intertwined with her skills, will one day give rise to the ultimate dance—one that embodies the heart and soul of the world.

How should I think about building hero gear for Yeonwoo?

Unlike many other hero roster builder games, Kingshot does not really have a mechanism by which you are rewarded for building hero gear differently for specific heroes like Yeonwoo. Hero gear is fully portable from one hero to another when you replace that hero in your roster and there are no synergies between hero skills and specific gear investments. (We've confirmed this through extensive testing and reverse engineering of the game's combat formulas.)

While it does not make sense to think of hero gear in terms of what works best for Yeonwoo specifically, there are considerations you should make around prioritizing hero gear investment for all archers like Yeonwoo. That is because different stats on different troop types have different values. For example, an extra point of health on infantry is much more valuable than an extra point of health on cavalry due to differences in troop base stats, combat simulation targeting logic, and other factors.

The implication of all this is that your question of what hero gear to build can be safely decoupled from the choice of hero to invest in. However, hero gear is still a question of supreme importance in Kingshot, and is in fact the question that gave Kingshot Optimizer its start as a website. So if you want to step up your game on the hero gear front, then check out the Hero Gear Optimizer today! It will allow you to see which gear piece you should prioritize next, give advice on reforging to more efficiently use your accumulated gear experience, and let you know where your next hero gear bottleneck comes from, e.g. a shortage of forgehammers. It takes into account the relative value of different stat points on different troop types, not just aggregate stat gain overall.

How do you validate the claims about Yeonwoo's skills made on this page?

Great question! You have a right to be skeptical given the amount of randomness of battles, the limited information available in battle reports, and the rampant rumors about heroes that spread throughout the Kingshot community. All of our claims are made from our own direct testing of Zebrave and Strat Game Sloth (the people behind this website) and Frak (of the Frakinator website). We do not simply repeat rumors unless we can provably verify them ourselves, which sometimes leads to our analysis feeling out of sync with conventional wisdom of these heroes. But we see this as a key differentiator of our work compared to other guides out there!

Our protocol follows a unit testing logic whereby we will isolate one hero ability at a time and run battles in-game with only tier 6 non-Truegold troops. We do this to remove any randomness from the native troop abilities. Because we have reverse engineered the relevant combat formulas into a simulator, we can predict the outcome of battles down to exact damage on a Bear Hunt or exact troop loss figures in a PvP match. You can see more about this applied to optimizing Bear Hunt troop ratios at the Strat Game Sloth video here: the Bear Hunt troop-ratio guide

Do all heroes eventually become available with Gold Keys?

Almost all heroes become available with Gold Keys, but there are some exceptions. In the Hero Hall (not to be confused with the "Hall of Heroes"), players can do an "Epic Recruitment" of heroes with the Gold Key item. At the start of the game, this only includes Jabel and the starter purple and blue heroes (except for Diana, which has her own event to acquire). When Generation 4 comes to your Kingdom, the Mythic heroes of Generation 2 (Marlin, Hilde, and Zoe) are added to the Gold Key loot pool when selecting Epic Recruitment. Then, when you reach Generation 5, the Generation 3 heroes (Petra, Eric, and Jaeger) are added to the Gold Key loot pool. This pattern continues where each new generation brings the heroes from two generations prior into the Gold Key loot pool.

The notable exceptions to this are the pay-to-win Generation 1 heroes Helga and Amadeus, and for some odd reason, Saul. While there are no ways besides pack purchases to get more Helga or Amadeus hero shards, you can at least continue building Saul by spending tickets (which can be purchased in limited quantities with gems) in the Hall of Heroes during future hero generations. This can be an alternative to using orange shards.

If you want to better understand when to save versus spend Gold Keys, then check out this Strat Game Sloth video on the topic: the Gold Keys guide
