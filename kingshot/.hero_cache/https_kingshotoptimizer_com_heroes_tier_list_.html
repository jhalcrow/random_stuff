How to Read This Tier List

Each Generation brings new heroes with new abilities. These abilities and each hero's relative stats are considered against a variety of roles that hero can fill for the player that lead to some sort of return on investment, such as Bear Hunt Rally Lead, Bear Hunt Joiner, Garrison Lead, etc.

There is a large diversity of what makes a hero good at one role versus another, hence there are multiple hero tier lists for each role. In some roles, especially joiner roles that rely on a single skill and no stats, even Generation 1 heroes can stay in the top tier. Therefore, we also show how the tier lists evolve over time and you can see how it looks for each hero generation.

For each of these roles, heroes are placed on a scale of tiers with Tier-S at the top, followed by Tier-A, Tier-B, Tier-C, and Tier-D, respectively. Tier placement is relative to the hero's performance during its generation of release versus other alternatives, and may therefore not always line up 1-to-1 with the star rating shown on hero cards. The difference between tiers is not always linear, and there is an irreducible element of judgement on where to draw the line.

A clear example of this sort of challenge can be seen in the PvP joiner tier list. In general, the flat 25% skills are ranked higher than the flat 20% skills due to their superiority from how the combat formula works. However, in a rally environment where skills of the same code stack additively and skills of different codes stack multiplicatively, a 20% skill with a rare code from a hero like Fahd or Eric can be just as valuable as a 25% skill from Gordon. So frequently the "best choice" of hero depends on context that simply cannot fit into a simple tier list.

Similarly, percent chance-based skills that don't stack at all can also be highly valuable in a rally provided there are no other copies of that hero to prevent stacking. So some tiers are not always "worse" as much as they are a call to "use the hero ability cautiously". This is why Petra actually moves up in the Bear Hunt joiner rally tier list in Generation 7 when she is bumped out of best-in-slot for Bear Hunt Rally Leader. She becomes less likely to already be in the rally, which makes her greater-than-25% expected value joiner skill potentially more useful with the right alliance coordination.

In any case, this should establish the complexity of the game doesn't play nicely with the concept of a tier list. We have attempted to balance this reality with the desire of the community to digest content in this format and landed on the resulting tier list. We hope it is a helpful community resource, but you should always feel welcome to provide suggestions on how it can be improved by reaching out to us on the methods listed on our contact page.

The Relevance of Hero Gear

Unlike many other hero roster builder games, Kingshot does not really have a mechanism by which you are rewarded for building hero gear differently for specific heroes. Hero gear is fully portable from one hero to another when you replace that hero in your roster and there are no synergies between hero skills and specific gear investments. (We've confirmed this through extensive testing and reverse engineering of the game's combat formulas.)

While it does not make sense to think of hero gear in terms of what works best for a given hero specifically, there are considerations you should make around prioritizing hero gear investment for, e.g. all infantry heroes. That is because different stats on different troop types have different values. For example, an extra point of health on infantry is much more valuable than an extra point of health on cavalry due to differences in troop base stats, combat simulation targeting logic, and other factors.

The implication of all this is that the question of what hero gear to build can be safely decoupled from the choice of hero to invest in. However, hero gear is still a question of supreme importance in Kingshot, and is in fact the question that gave Kingshot Optimizer its start as a website! So if you want to step up your game on the hero gear front, then check out the Hero Gear Optimizer today. It will allow you to see which gear piece you should prioritize next, give advice on reforging to more efficiently use your accumulated gear experience, and let you know where your next hero gear bottleneck comes from, e.g. a shortage of forgehammers. It takes into account the relative value of different stat points on different troop types, not just aggregate stat gain overall.

How The Kingshot Hero Landscape Evolves by Generation

The following is an overview of how each hero generation changes the game with its introduction. If you want to see more details about a given hero, then check out their hero card as well as their hero details page.

For more details on the exact day each hero generation (and other gameplay features) will appear in your Kingdom, simply enter in your Kingdom number in our Kingdom Timeline module.

Generation 1: The Foundation of City Growth and Initial Meta

Generation 1 comes with 16 heroes that are split into rare (blue), epic (purple), and mythic (orange). This starter generation is characterized by heroes designed to accelerate account progression and establish fundamental combat strategies. Strategic growth is anchored by Diana, who is mandatory for new players because her skills reduce stamina costs and double march speed, allowing for a higher volume of resources and materials from PvE events. Similarly, Saul provides essential long-term value via his "Resourceful" ability, which reduces construction time and costs by up to 15%. Economy specialists like Olive, Forrest, Edwin, and Seth offer limited Arena utility but primarily serve to increase food, wood, stone, and iron production long-term.

Generation 1 also introduces the core stacking meta for joiner heroes. Chenko, Yeonwoo, and Amane are essential joiners for offensive rallies, providing a 25% offensive stat buff that stacks with other alliance members, which is critical for Bear Hunt. Conversely, Gordon is the premier defensive specialist, offering a 25% health increase that outperforms the 20% damage reduction seen in heroes like Howard or Quinn, but Fahd's 20% skill is still competitive due to the multiplicative effect of the rare skill code.

For high-spending players, Amadeus and Helga define the early "whale" meta. Amadeus is the unchallenged king of Bear Hunt (and other offensive) rally leading and functions as a decisive melee DPS/tank hybrid in Arena. Helga provides critical early-game Arena support with crowd control via her knockback and stun ultimate, though she is quickly outclassed by later generations. Finally, Jabel remains the most accessible mythic hero, serving as a best-in-slot cavalry rally leader for both free-to-play and spending players due to her high stats.

Generation 2: The Fast Follow Wave of New Heroes

Generation 2 heroes arrive quickly, only day 37-50 depending on how your Kingdom's calendar aligns with the real life calendar. It represents a significant statistical leap over many Generation 1 counterparts since all new heroes from this point forward are mythic rarity. Zoe is the standout infantry hero, accessible via the Hero Roulette. She sets a new standard for front-line durability in Arena, being the first hero to exceed the baseline 2,220 defense. Her strategic value in Conquest comes from "Shield Strike," which amplifies damage taken by opponents, and a self-heal that triggers when she falls below half-health. In Expedition, she is an offensive infantry powerhouse for F2P players, though her chance-based skills like "Sundering Wound" require caution in rallies as they do not stack.

Marlin becomes the definitive offensive archer specialist, widely considered best-in-slot for Bear Hunt and offensive rallies due to his "Admiral of the Line" exclusive gear, which provides a multiplicative 15% lethality bonus. In Arena, Marlin acts as a leading disruptor, dealing wide AoE damage and applying a 1.5-second stun. Hilde introduces impactful team-wide healing to the Arena meta, utilizing her signature move to neutralize opposing ultimates and extend her frontline's uptime. While she is a slight upgrade over Jabel for defensive garrison leading in Expedition, her high building cost makes her a lower priority for F2P players compared to Marlin or Zoe.

Generation 3: The Defensive Power Creep

Generation 3 triggers a massive shift in Arena meta as all heroes receive a 50% increase in base defense over all prior generations (except Zoe, who got a small boost in Gen 2). Eric becomes the new premier infantry tank for top-tier formations, boasting elite crowd control that reduces opposing attack speed. His primary Expedition utility is as a top garrison leader, but his long-term utility comes from a unique joiner ability skill code that reduces opposing attack by 20%, which can stack in rallies to cripple even the strongest offensive opponents. Jaeger serves as an elite archer garrison leader, but players won't use him for much else in Expedition mode. In Arena, he is a back-row disruptor with the highest attack stat of his generation, an odd design choice. His "Sound of Silence" exclusive gear ability can prevent opposing skills for 5 seconds, but he can sometimes heal opponents with his "Game of Chance" skill.

Petra is the generation's Roulette cavalry hero, defined by high-risk, high-reward randomness. In Arena, her abilities can fluctuate between huge damage and huge heals… or nothing at all, making her volatile without significant widget investment to raise her "minimum floor". However, she shines in Bear Hunt; intensive testing revealed her "Evil Eye" skill has a much higher chance of triggering than her skill description suggests, making her the strongest cavalry choice for Bear Hunt rally leading through multiple subsequent generations.

Generation 4: Rise of the 60/40 Meta

Generation 4 heroes introduce troop-specific buffs that enable the powerful "60/40" (infantry/cavalry) expedition formation meta. Alcar is the featured infantry hero who transforms infantry from meat shields into viable damage dealers in expedition mode, while providing a team-wide 70% damage reduction for 2 seconds in Arena, which is frequently timed to neutralize opposing ultimates. Margot is the cornerstone cavalry hero for defensive garrisons using 60/40 (or any formation), offering a unique "Subterfuge" ability that provides a 20% chance to entirely nullify damage. In Arena, she is the premier AoE damage dealer, possessing the second-highest attack of her generation and a signature move with a massive 420% damage multiplier.

Rosa is the Roulette archer hero and a mandatory upgrade for Bear Hunt, outperforming Marlin due to her massive base stat advantage and with the same offensive widget. Despite her Expedition dominance, she is considered the weakest Arena hero of the generation; her skills lack AoE to take advantage of her stats, often functioning as a single-target disruptor whose primary value is simply her high base attack.

Generation 5: Skill Complexity and Backline Targeting

Generation 5 introduces the most complex hero abilities to date, focusing on unique targeting logic and self-immunity. Vivian is a meta-shifting archer who introduces the "Gilded Barrage" in Arena, which bypasses frontline tanks to strike back-row damage dealers with 252% AoE damage. In Expedition, she is a defensive garrison specialist with a skill that changes the usual troop targeting order in simulated battles. Thrud allows cavalry to damage all three troop types simultaneously in Expedition combat as well, a significant strategic shift for dislodging fortified PvP garrisons. Her random-targeting axe flurry in Arena pressures the opposing backline early too, bypassing traditional meat shields.

Long Fei is the Roulette infantry hero, upgrading the F2P Arena tank slot with control immunity and damage-based self-healing. His exclusive gear skill "Strong Brew" allows him to restore health based on damage dealt, making him a "win more" hero who performs best when he possesses a stat advantage. While his Expedition skills narrow the distribution of outcomes for defenders, he notably underperforms Alcar and Zoe in Bear Hunt leading because he possesses only one offensive skill compared to their two.

Generation 6: Compounding Power and Status Effect Complexity

Generation 6 brings the largest absolute stat increases in the game's history due to compounding growth on the % increase. Yang is the standout archer hero, perfectly aligned for Bear Hunt with an offensive lethality widget and multiplicative damage skills that lead to a significant score increase over Rosa. In Arena, her signature snowboard attack hits targets across the field twice, and her critical hit mechanic acts as a fatal blow to escort troops.

Sophia is the first free-to-play friendly defensive cavalry hero since Generation 1 and introduces the "confusion" status effect, which redirects opponents' attacks onto their own allies. This makes her Arena kit highly scalable as it uses the opponent's own strength against them. Her Expedition skill kit also has a "Terror" status effect, but this largely only impacts the way damage is calculated like other hero abilities due to battles being simulated.

Triton functions as a massive defensive anchor with the highest base health and defense in the game during his generation. However, his Expedition kit contains a misleading "red herring" skill that appears to boost damage but only affects "active" hero abilities while ignoring common passive buffs like Chenko's. While his raw stats ensure he remains in top-tier Arena teams, he is primarily only necessary for high spenders responsible for alliance garrisons.

Generation 7: The Largest Absolute Power Leap

Generation 7 represents the largest power jump to date, offering up to a +110% improvement to Expedition stats and a 20% increase to all arena stats. Ava is a huge asset for Bear Hunt, an offensive cavalry powerhouse with three damage-increasing skills and an offensive widget. This combination is exceptionally rare and ensures long-term relevance even after her base stats are eventually surpassed (due to the low fraction of cavalry found in Bear Hunt). Wee & Woo are the Roulette archer pair, offering a "broken" Arena kit with AoE basic attacks and multiple field-wide buffs that devastate formations. They are also a best-in-slot upgrade for 60/40 garrison defenses and can even do Bear Hunt in a pinch despite the wrong widget type.

Charles continues the trend of defensive infantry with a defensive widget, but is initially viewed with caution by theorycrafters because his primary skills are capped at 20%. At least one of them seems to have an uncommon skill code. While his raw Generation 7 stats often offset these mechanical shortcomings, he remains a niche investment for garrison leaders, with many players advised to wait for the potential infantry hero in the Generation 8 Roulette. Overall, Generation 7 is defined by its massive absolute strength leap, making these heroes mandatory for players looking to modernize their competitive Arena, PvP, and Bear Hunt capabilities.

The Free-to-Play and Low-Spender Investment Priority Path

Free-to-play Kingshot players and low spenders must make careful use of their limited hero shards and widgets. Accordingly, it is important to know which heroes are worth the investment, especially mythic heroes. Accordingly, here is some light guidance, but feel free to deviate to match your style of gameplay and spending level comfort. Additional in-depth hero reviews and investment scenarios for all types of Kingshot players can be found in this playlist.

For Generation 1, obviously build Jabel since she is the only farmable mythic hero that generation. You should consider getting a few star levels into Saul to unlock levels of his growth skill, but don't over-invest since Generation 1 is short. Obviously you will build all of the blue and purple heroes eventually, but that's a matter of what to invest in first. In addition to their hero pages, you can check out this Strat Game Sloth video for a priority list of purple heroes.

For Generation 2, build Zoe from the Hero Roulette wheel since she will be a big upgrade over Howard. If you have the orange shards, you should consider building Marlin as well for improvement to Bear Hunt, but it will be tough for free-to-play players to get enough in time and the widgets to build his exclusive gear.

In Generation 3, you should grab Petra from the Roulette wheel. She will be the best-in-slot cavalry hero for Bear Hunt until Generation 7. It is safe to skip Eric and Jaeger if you are not planning on defending garrisons in PvP content such as Castle Battle.

In Generation 4, you will likely have the orange shards saved from only building Petra last generation. In addition to building Rosa from the Hero Roulette wheel (to improve your Bear Hunt score), you can consider taking either Alcar or Margot. Both are amazing in Arena and have unique abilities they bring into Expedition combat, so this one is a matter of preference.

Generation 5 brings Long Fei on the Hero Roulette wheel. Since he is free, he is worth building for bolstering your teams in multi-team content like Mystic Trial, Tri-Alliance Clash, and Swordland Showdown, but he won't replace Zoe or Alcar on Bear Hunt. Thrud and Vivian are amazing for Arena and have some unique Expedition skills, but free-to-play players who are not building for PvP won't get much use out of them. The uniqueness of Thrud's abilities also means she is not as well suited to Bear Hunt as Petra. If you didn't get Petra for some reason, then she can be potentially worth building.

Generation 6 features Sophia on the wheel. Similar to Long Fei, she is a defensive widget hero who does not really help you in Bear Hunt. Again, it's the same logic, she can help in multi-team content like Mystic Trial, Tri-Alliance Clash, and Swordland Showdown, but that's about as much return as you will get for her. Triton can be safely skipped if you have Long Fei since he doesn't add much new. Yang is the hero you should consider using orange shards on due to her amazing utility in Bear Hunt.

Generation 7 has two heroes worth considering, and one of them is on the Hero Roulette wheel. Wee & Woo is the Roulette hero, and all free-to-play players should build him due to amazing abilities. If you did not build Yang in generation 6, he may even be your best choice for Bear Hunt despite the defensive widget. Ava is amazing for Bear Hunt and finally replaces Petra. It looks like she may have the same sort of longevity as Petra did. Charles can probably be safely skipped as the Generation 8 wheel hero is expected to be infantry.

Hero Tier List FAQs

The most common questions from new and intermediate Kingshot players relate to which heroes are worth investing in, and how should you think about hero priority over the long run. The “which heroes to invest in” question is answered by the tier lists above, but let’s talk more broadly about hero investment as a whole.

When is the right time to stop investing in a hero?

A key signal is usually the arrival of next hero generation. Each new generation of heroes is typically stronger per shard investment than the one before. (Although there are some exceptions for certain use cases, as you can see from the tier list.) Therefore, the opportunity cost of continuing to pour shards into an older hero goes up every time a new gen drops. It helps to think of heroes as depreciating assets (like a car), not because the heroes get weaker, but because their relative power declines as newer and stronger options arrive. Once a newer, better hero is available and you haven't maxed out the older one, it's usually better to pivot as a default choice, unless you have a specific role you have in mind for that old hero that is still relevant.

Is it worth building a hero past 4 stars?

4 stars unlocks all of a hero's skills, which is a major power breakpoint for every hero. Meanwhile, the 5th star gives significant additional stats, but the cost in shards is also significant compared to the gain. (You can see this in the Hero Shard calculator module.)

There is not a one-size-fits-all answer to this question. If you are building a hero for something where only the skills matter and not the stats (such as a rally joiner hero, or a Bear Hunt lead hero of infantry or cavalry), then you don’t get much return for that 5th star. If you can make good use of the stats (e.g. because you are a spender expecting to lead rallies or because you are going to use this archer hero for Bear Hunt), then it might make sense.

Should I invest in the new generation heroes right when they launch, or finish the previous ones?

Generally, you should pivot your shard investments to new generation heroes when they launch, but there are exception. Each new generation is designed to be stronger than the previous one, so the power per shard invested is higher for the newest heroes. That said, if you're one or two investments away from unlocking a key skill on an older hero (especially approaching 4 stars), it can be worth finishing that threshold first before switching focus. It could also make sense if you are continuing to build the widget of an older hero that you expect to use for many generations in Bear Hunt because there are some opportunities to pick up prior generation widgets for cheap.

What should I do with mythic (custom) orange hero shards early game?

Save them! The instinct when you first get mythic shards is to dump them into Saul since he is the only hero besides Jabel (with freely available shards) that you can build. But the first generation of heroes is extremely fast, and soon you will have more options to choose from. Saving custom orange shards so you can unlock or boost a later generation hero, one that would otherwise require spending to build, is almost always a better long-term use. Spend gems freely on the hero wheel to build heroes when the opportunity arises, but save the mythic general hero shards for heroes that are hard to get any other way.
